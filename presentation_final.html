Objectives

Three goals of this study

1

Quantify the marginal effects of weather

Wind speed, rainfall, temperature, humidity — estimating the full 95% credible interval, not just point values, to map the uncertainty.

2

Compare three models of increasing complexity

Using the Deviance Information Criterion (DIC) and verifying accurate out-of-sample predictions on a 90-day hold-out.

3

Rigorously validate the chosen model

Full set of Posterior Predictive Checks and formal residual analysis, to ensure statistical soundness.

Why log-transform: from skewed to symmetric

The raw distribution is strongly right-skewed (mean > median); on the log scale the two coincide → symmetric, suitable for a Normal/$t$ likelihood.

Models

Three competing models

Model 1 — Independent linear regression
\[ Y_t = \alpha + \sum_{k=1}^{4} \beta_k X_{t,k} + \varepsilon_t, \quad \varepsilon_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2) \]

Baseline. Assumes time independence — ignores the chronological structure.

Model 2 — AR(1) with weather covariates
\[ Y_t = \alpha + \boxed{\phi\, Y_{t-1}} + \sum_{k=1}^{4} \beta_k X_{t,k} + \varepsilon_t, \quad \varepsilon_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2), \quad |\phi| < 1 \]

Adds time persistence: yesterday's pollution enters today's equation.

Model 3 — AR(1) + seasonality + Student-$t$
\[ Y_t = \alpha + \phi Y_{t-1} + \sum_k \beta_k X_{t,k} + \boxed{\gamma_1 \sin\!\big(\tfrac{2\pi\,\text{doy}_t}{365.25}\big) + \gamma_2 \cos\!\big(\tfrac{2\pi\,\text{doy}_t}{365.25}\big)} + \varepsilon_t \] \[ \varepsilon_t \sim \boxed{t_\nu(0, \sigma^2)} \]

Seasonal cycle via two harmonics + heavy tails via Student-$t$ with unknown $\nu$ → extreme peaks become structural signal, not outliers.

Physical interpretation

  • $\phi = 0.55$: yesterday alone explains half of today's variability.
  • Wind: +1 sd → $\times e^{-0.11} \approx 0.89$ ($-11\%$).
  • Rain: negative (wet deposition).
  • Temp +0.22: positive within season → stagnation.
  • $\nu \approx 4$: heavy tails are structural.
Sign reversal of Temp = textbook example of how seasonality removes omitted-variable bias.

Adequacy check

Posterior Predictive Checks

Generate 2 000 replicated series from the posterior, compute summary statistics, compare with the observed value. The Bayes $p$-value is the share of replicates more extreme than the observation — ideally $\approx 0.5$.

statisticobserved M2 normalM3 t-Student
mean25.7 0.51 ✓ 0.57 ✓
standard deviation13.2 0.150.68
maximum (µg/m³) 142.0 0.06 × 0.62 ✓
share > 50 µg/m³7.7% 0.54 ✓ 0.68 ✓
M2 fails on the maximum (Bayes $p = 0.06$): the Gaussian cannot generate extreme winter peaks.

M3 fixes it (Bayes $p = 0.62$): the observed maximum sits at the centre of the replicated distribution.

Red line: observed value. Grey histogram: replicated values across 2 000 posterior draws.

Empirical justification of the Student-$t$ — not academic flair, statistical necessity.

Take-home message

  • PM10 in Rome is persistent ($\phi \approx 0.55$)…
  • … with deep seasonality (winter/summer ratio $\approx 2$)…
  • … driven by wind dispersion and rainfall washout…
  • … and heavy-tailed extremes with $\nu \approx 4$.
